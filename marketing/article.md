# Hyperpad: the launchpad where trading pays for the marketing

*Suggested cover: `images/hyperpad-header.png`. Replace `[CA]` and `[link]` before publishing.*

Most coins launch the same way. Someone makes a token, posts the contract address, and then spends the next week trying to get anyone to talk about it. The launch is the easy part. Attention is the hard part, and it usually costs money the launcher does not have.

Hyperpad starts from a different idea: a coin's own trading should pay for its marketing.

## How it works

Launching takes about a minute. You pick a name, a ticker, one line of pitch and one image. The coin goes live with its own card in the Hyperpad feed.

From then on, every trade pays a 1% fee, split three ways:

- **0.3% to you**, the launcher, paid to your wallet in ETH.
- **0.5% to the coin's reel jar.**
- **0.2% to Hyperpad.**

When the reel jar reaches $25, Hyperpad books a new short video about your coin. An AI host presents it to camera in one of eight formats: a talking head, an unboxing, a street interview, a hot take, a reaction, an explainer, a skit or a podcast clip. Rendering takes about eight minutes, and the reel goes straight to the feed.

Formats rotate, so a coin never gets the same one twice in a row. Hosts change too. Ten reels in, it looks like ten different people found your coin.

## The feed is the chart

The Hyperpad feed has one rule. The newest reel plays first, full screen, with a buy button under it.

That makes the feed a live picture of which coins people are trading. A coin that keeps trading keeps filling its jar and keeps getting new reels. A coin that stops trading stops getting reels and drops out of view. Nobody curates it. Volume is the algorithm.

## No prompts, no real people

Reels are built from fixed formats using only four inputs: the coin's name, its ticker, its pitch line and its image. There are no free-text prompts, no real people and no third-party brands in the videos. The hosts are AI, and every reel says so.

Launchers own the reels made for their coin and can post them anywhere: X, TikTok, Instagram, their own site.

## Tips in ZEC

Trading is not the only way to fund a reel. Any Hyperpad coin can take tips in Zcash, and every tip goes straight into the coin's reel jar.

We picked ZEC for a reason. A shielded Zcash tip hides the sender, the receiver and the amount on chain, so fans can back a coin without putting their wallet history on display. Shielded tips can also carry an encrypted memo of up to 512 bytes, which only the coin's owner can read. Fans use it to say hi, or to ask for a format: "make the next one an unboxing."

Tipping works with any Zcash wallet. Hyperpad shows a QR code carrying a standard ZIP-321 payment request with the amount and memo already filled in.

## What $25 buys

To put numbers on it: a coin trading $10,000 a day pays $100 in fees. Of that, $30 goes to the launcher, $50 buys two reels, and $20 goes to Hyperpad. At $100,000 a day, the same coin earns its launcher $300 and gets twenty reels.
